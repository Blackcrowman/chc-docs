2. Why You Can & Should Claim

NHS Continuing Healthcare (CHC) is a complete package of care funded entirely by the NHS for anyone over 18 with a 'primary health need'. Unlike social care through the council, CHC is completely free and never means-tested against life savings or property.

Stop Paying

If your claim is for CHC and successful, the NHS steps in. The claimant stops paying care home fees, saving thousands of pounds every single month.

The Backstory

Back in the 1990s, when the NHS sold off its care homes and long-stay geriatric hospitals, the state kept a strict legal duty and a ring-fenced national budget currently standing at around £6.5 billion a year to fund care driven by medical needs.

Don't Feel Guilty

You aren't taking money away from local hospitals, doctors, or A&E. This dedicated NHS budget is purely for health-based care. If they qualify, that money was always meant to cover their care. They are completely entitled to it.

Mental Health Act & Section 117 (Free Care Rules)

If the claimant spent time detained in hospital for mental health treatment (under Section 3 of the Mental Health Act) and was released to a care home, the law says they were entitled to free aftercare the moment they left.

This means the NHS and council have to foot the entire bill. If they went straight from that hospital stay into a care home for mental health support, that funding is locked in for life, no time limits, and no means-testing allowed. Any care home fees paid out during that time were taken unlawfully, which means every single penny can be claimed back. NHS bodies frequently ignore historical Section 117 duties, meaning your formal record requests act as strict legal leverage.

Action Point: Dig through old medical files or social work notes for "Section 3" or "Section 117". Finding it is basically hitting the jackpot for a full refund.

3. Understanding PUPoC

PUPoC (Previously Unassessed Period of Care) means looking back at past care periods where the claimant should have been assessed for CHC, but the ICB never bothered. NHS bodies routinely drag their feet, lose records, or fail to investigate unless formally pressured. Securing your clinical files immediately using statutory Subject Access Requests (SAR) or Access to Health Records Act (AHRA) requests establishes binding legal deadlines and prevents vital evidence from degrading or disappearing.

What's It Worth?

Because care home fees are typically £1,000 to £4,000+ per week, a successful PUPoC claim for past months or years frequently totals tens or hundreds of thousands of pounds in refunds.

Crucial Date Cut-Off

The absolute earliest date you can claim a refund for is 1 April 2012. Because of an NHS cut-off rule, any periods of care before that date are officially closed off to standard claims. So if the claimant was in care anytime after this you can claim from the 1st day of care.

4. What They All Mean
  • DST (Decision Support Tool): The standardised assessment document used to record evidence across the key indicators.
  • MDT (Multi-Disciplinary Team): A group of health and social care professionals who jointly complete the assessment and make eligibility recommendations.
  • ICB (Integrated Care Board): The NHS statutory body responsible for commissioning healthcare services, evaluating CHC eligibility, and ultimately paying out.
  • CHC (NHS Continuing Healthcare): A fully funded package of health and social care provided free of charge by the NHS under the National Framework.
  • PUPoC (Previously Unassessed Periods of Care): Retrospective claims for historical care periods where no CHC assessment took place.
  • SAR (Subject Access Request): A formal legal request under UK data protection legislation to obtain historical records from healthcare providers and local authorities. Must be answered within one calendar month.
  • AHRA (Access to Health Records Act 1990): A formal legal request under UK legislation that gives specific people the legal right to view the health records of someone who has died. Must be answered in 40 calendar days.
  • PSO (Public Services Ombudsman): Formerly known as the PHSO, this is the final independent tier for unresolved complaints against NHS bodies.
